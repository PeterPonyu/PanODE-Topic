\Title{Flow-Matching-Refined Dirichlet-Prior Autoencoders for Interpretable and Structurally Balanced Single-Cell Representation Learning}

\Author{Zeyu Fu $^{1,*}$}

\AuthorNames{Zeyu Fu}

\address{%

%=================================================================
\abstract{We introduce Topic-FM, a family of flow-matching-refined Dirichlet-prior variational autoencoders for single-cell transcriptomics. A conditional optimal-transport velocity field, trained in pre-softmax $\mathbb{R}^K$ space, sharpens the logistic-normal posterior without violating the simplex constraint, yielding latent representations that jointly improve label concordance, cluster geometry, and biological interpretability. We evaluate four architectural variants---Topic-FM-Base (MLP), Topic-FM-Transformer (self-attention), Topic-FM-Contrastive (MoCo), and Topic-FM-GAT (graph attention over $k$NN)---on 56 scRNA-seq datasets. Topic-FM-Transformer achieves the highest composite score (NMI\,+\,ARI\,+\,ASW\,/\,3 = 0.502), improving over the prior-free Pure-VAE baseline by 15.6\%, with concordant gains in NMI (+8.2\%), ARI (+20.4\%), and ASW (+21.7\%); Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm significance with medium-to-large Cliff's $\delta$ effects. In a full external comparison against 23 published baselines, Topic-FM-Contrastive wins 86.4\% of core-metric comparisons ($p{<}0.05$). Biological validation shows that the learned topics correspond to coherent Gene Ontology programs through both perturbation-based and decoder-weight-based enrichment. By uniting the Dirichlet prior's built-in interpretability with flow-based posterior refinement, Topic-FM provides a practical and interpretable default for single-cell representation learning.}

\keyword{single-cell RNA-seq; variational autoencoder; Dirichlet prior; flow matching; optimal transport; topic model; representation learning; interpretability}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) generates high-dimensional, sparse count matrices that require compact latent representations for clustering, trajectory inference, and cell-type annotation~\cite{wolf2018scanpy}. Variational autoencoders (VAEs)~\cite{kingma2014auto} have become a standard tool for this task---notably scVI~\cite{lopez2018deep} and its derivatives~\cite{gayoso2022python}---yet the conventional isotropic Gaussian prior imposes no structure on the latent space and offers no direct mapping from latent dimensions to gene programs.

Replacing the Gaussian prior with a logistic-normal approximation to a Dirichlet distribution constrains the latent vector to a probability simplex ($\Delta^{K-1}$), converting the VAE into a neural topic model~\cite{srivastava2017autoencoding,miao2016neural}. This reparameterisation yields three concrete advantages. \emph{First}, each latent dimension represents a soft topic allocation, interpretable as a cell's membership weight across $K$ gene programs. \emph{Second}, the decoder contains an explicit topic--gene matrix $\beta$ whose rows map topics to gene-expression probabilities, enabling biological annotation without post-hoc importance scoring. \emph{Third}, the Dirichlet prior's sparsity-promoting concentration parameters encourage compact, well-separated clusters that improve both label concordance and cluster geometry relative to prior-free baselines.

Despite these strengths, the logistic-normal variational posterior may under-represent fine-grained geometric structure in the pre-softmax space. We address this limitation with \emph{conditional flow matching} (CFM)~\cite{lipman2023flow,tong2024improving}: a lightweight velocity field, trained via optimal-transport interpolation~\cite{villani2009optimal} in $\mathbb{R}^K$, refines posterior samples at inference time, sharpening cluster boundaries without violating the simplex constraint. The flow matching module activates after a warmup period and adds negligible computational overhead.

We evaluate four flow-matching-refined Dirichlet VAE variants---Topic-FM-Base (feedforward), Topic-FM-Transformer (self-attention), Topic-FM-Contrastive (MoCo contrastive), and Topic-FM-GAT (graph attention)---on 56 scRNA-seq datasets. Topic-FM-Transformer achieves the highest composite score (0.502, +15.6\% over Pure-VAE), with concordant gains across NMI, ARI, and ASW. Combined with built-in interpretability via the decoder $\beta$ matrix and the strongest downstream $k$NN classification accuracy, these results position the flow-matching-refined Dirichlet prior as a practical and interpretable default for single-cell representation learning.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Model Architecture}
We evaluate four flow-matching-refined Dirichlet VAE variants, each paired with a matched prior-free baseline.

\textbf{Topic-FM-Base.} A two-layer MLP encoder ($128 \to 128$) maps input counts to a logistic-normal posterior over $K{=}10$ topics: $\log\theta = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$, $\theta = \text{softmax}(\log\theta)$. A learned decoder matrix $\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times G}$, softmax-normalised per topic, reconstructs gene probabilities as $P(\text{gene} \mid \theta) = \theta^\top \beta$, providing an explicit topic--gene mapping.

\textbf{Topic-FM-Transformer.} The MLP encoder is replaced by multi-head self-attention (4 heads) in a cell-as-token design, enabling the model to attend to heterogeneous cell properties within each batch and capture cell--cell interaction patterns.

\textbf{Topic-FM-Contrastive.} A MoCo-v2~\cite{chen2020improved} contrastive head with stochastic augmentation (Bernoulli dropout, additive noise) and a 4096-entry memory queue ($\tau{=}0.2$) is added, combining instance-level discrimination with the Dirichlet prior.

\textbf{Topic-FM-GAT.} A Graph Attention Network~\cite{velickovic2018graph} encoder operates over a precomputed $k$-nearest-neighbour graph ($k{=}15$). Two GATConv layers with 4 attention heads, residual connections, and LayerNorm aggregate neighbourhood information, allowing the model to exploit local transcriptomic similarity.

All variants share the same training objective. KL weight is fixed at 0.01 with free bits to prevent posterior collapse; Dirichlet concentration strength $\kappa{=}10$. Pure-VAE, Pure-Transformer-VAE, and Pure-Contrastive-VAE serve as matched prior-free baselines.

\subsection{Flow Matching Refinement}
After a warmup phase ($T_\text{warm}{=}50$ epochs), a conditional optimal-transport (OT) flow field is jointly trained in the pre-softmax space $\mathbb{R}^K$. Given a posterior sample $\log\theta$ (target) and standard Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ (source), the OT interpolant is $z_t = (1{-}t)\epsilon + t\log\theta$ with velocity target $v^* = \log\theta - \epsilon$. A small MLP $v_\psi(z_t, t)$ is trained to minimise
\[
  \mathcal{L}_\text{FM} = \mathbb{E}_{t\sim U(0,1)} \bigl\| v_\psi(z_t, t) - v^* \bigr\|^2.
\]
The total loss combines the ELBO and flow objectives: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{ELBO} + \lambda_\text{FM}\,\mathcal{L}_\text{FM}$ ($\lambda_\text{FM}{=}0.1$). At inference, partial Euler integration from $t_0{=}0.8$ to $t{=}1.0$ in 10 steps denoises the posterior sample before softmax projection, sharpening cluster boundaries while preserving the simplex constraint. The velocity network uses sinusoidal time embeddings (dim\,=\,32), two hidden layers ($128 \to 128$), LayerNorm, and Mish activations.

\subsection{Training and Data}
Default hyperparameters are: KL weight 0.01, dropout 0.0, weight decay $10^{-3}$, learning rate $10^{-3}$, 1000 epochs, batch size 128. Flow matching activates at epoch 50 with $\lambda_\text{FM}{=}0.1$. Each dataset is preprocessed by selecting the top 3000 highly variable genes (HVGs), applying library-size normalisation and $\log(1{+}x)$ transformation, and subsampling to at most 3000 cells. The benchmark catalogue comprises 56 datasets (16 core cohorts plus 40 additional preprocessed collections spanning haematopoiesis, neural, endodermal, and immune tissues).

\subsection{Evaluation}
We adopt a multi-metric protocol centred on four core clustering metrics.

\textbf{Label concordance.}
Normalised Mutual Information (NMI) and Adjusted Rand Index (ARI)~\cite{hubert1985comparing} are computed by running $K$-means ($K$ = number of ground-truth labels) on the latent vectors.

\textbf{Geometric structure.}
Average Silhouette Width (ASW, $\uparrow$)~\cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}, Davies--Bouldin Index (DAV, $\downarrow$)~\cite{davies1979cluster}, Calinski--Harabasz Index (CAL, $\uparrow$), and latent correlation (COR, $\uparrow$).

Dimensionality Reduction Evaluation (DRE) and Latent Structure Evaluation (LSE) families, together with their extended variants DREX/LSEX, are reported for completeness but \emph{excluded from Topic-FM composite scores}. The simplex-constrained latent space ($\Delta^{K-1}$) has bounded pairwise distances and rank-deficient covariance, making distance-preservation metrics non-comparable across prior families.

The composite score used for ranking is $\text{Score} = (\text{NMI} + \text{ARI} + \text{ASW}) / 3$.

Topic-FM-Contrastive vs.\ Pure-Contrastive-VAE wins on each of the four core metrics (12/0/0; $p = 2.44\times10^{-4}$; Cliff's $\delta = 0.81$--$1.00$~\cite{cliff1993dominance}, all large effects). \subsection{Downstream Classification}
Table~\ref{tab:topic_knn} reports $k$NN classification accuracy and macro-F1 as a direct downstream test of representation quality.

\input{tables/knn}

Topic-FM-Transformer achieves the best accuracy (0.733) and macro-F1 (0.517), exceeding Pure-VAE by 13.5\% and 27.7\%, respectively. Every Topic-FM variant outperforms every Pure-VAE variant, confirming that the Dirichlet prior with flow matching refinement consistently improves discriminative representation quality.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis}
To assess robustness, we sweep nine hyperparameters (KL weight, dropout, weight decay, learning rate, epochs, batch size, latent dimension, encoder size, HVG count) across 12 datasets and four core metrics. Topic-FM-Base maintains stable performance over a broad range of values, confirming that the default hyperparameters are not heavily tuned. The most sensitive parameter is KL weight, where values below $10^{-3}$ or above $10^{-1}$ degrade ARI; the remaining parameters show flat response curves across one to two orders of magnitude of variation.

\subsection{Biological Validation and Interpretability}

The Dirichlet prior affords a dual-pathway biological validation unavailable to methods lacking a topic--gene decoder matrix. \emph{Pathway~1}: perturbation-based gene-importance scoring identifies genes whose ablation most affects each latent component. \emph{Pathway~2}: the decoder $\beta$ weights directly read out gene-program membership from the learned parameters. Because the flow matching module operates in pre-softmax space, it does not alter $\beta$ and fully preserves this interpretability.

We validate both pathways on three representative datasets (setty, endo, dentate). Perturbation importance and decoder $\beta$ weights independently identify distinct gene sets per topic: $Z$-scored importance heatmaps reveal non-overlapping gene modules across topics, and the decoder $\beta$ rows show sparse, complementary activation patterns. Latent--gene Pearson correlations (top 30 genes per topic by $|r|$) exhibit block-diagonal structure, confirming that each topic captures a distinct expression programme. Per-component UMAP projections demonstrate spatial separation of known cell types through topic-weighted colouring. Gene Ontology enrichment analysis~\cite{ashburner2000gene} on the top 20 salient genes per topic yields significant biological-process terms (Fisher's exact test, Bonferroni-corrected $p < 0.05$) under both the perturbation and decoder-$\beta$ pathways, providing convergent evidence that the learned topics correspond to coherent gene regulatory programmes.

\subsection{External Benchmark}
The metric profiles of the four Topic-FM variants remain consistent when evaluated alongside 23 external baselines on 16 core datasets (the same ranking pattern as in the internal ablation of Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}). Table~\ref{tab:topic_external_winrate} quantifies external win rates via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.

\input{tables/external_winrate}

Topic-FM-Contrastive achieves the highest core win rate (86.4\%), winning 38 of 44 core-metric comparisons ($p < 0.05$). All Topic-FM variants surpass all Pure-VAE variants on core win rate, confirming that the Dirichlet prior's advantage generalises beyond internal baselines. ASW is the strongest axis, with near-universal wins against external models, whereas NMI shows the fewest significant wins---scVI~\cite{lopez2018deep} and CLEAR~\cite{han2022clear} achieve higher mean concordance on individual datasets. No single external model matches the combined concordance--geometry--interpretability--downstream profile of Topic-FM.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Concordant gains across metric axes.}
The central finding is that the flow-matching-refined Dirichlet prior improves label concordance and cluster geometry simultaneously rather than trading one for the other. Topic-FM-Transformer raises NMI (+8.2\%), ARI (+20.4\%), and ASW (+21.7\%) over Pure-VAE in a single model, with Wilcoxon tests confirming medium-to-large effect sizes on all three metrics. This concordant profile makes Topic-FM a practical default when both clustering quality and structural coherence are required.

\textbf{Flow matching as posterior refinement.}
The conditional OT velocity field operates entirely in pre-softmax $\mathbb{R}^K$ space, sharpening cluster boundaries via partial Euler integration ($t_0{=}0.8 \to 1.0$) without breaking the simplex constraint after softmax projection. Because it is trained with a simple MSE loss and activates only after the VAE has converged, it avoids interference with early-stage posterior optimisation and adds minimal computational cost.

\textbf{Variant specialisation.}
Table~\ref{tab:topic_variant_ranking} ranks the individual variants.
Topic-FM-Transformer leads on composite score, NMI, and ASW, benefiting from self-attention's ability to capture inter-cell correlations within each batch. Topic-FM-Contrastive achieves the highest ARI, best DAV, and the top external win rate (86.4\%), indicating that contrastive learning synergises with the Dirichlet prior for the strongest generalisation. Topic-FM-Base offers the simplest architecture with a 9.8\% composite gain and negligible overhead. Topic-FM-GAT provides a graph-based alternative that exploits local transcriptomic similarity via $k$NN attention.

\input{tables/variant_ranking}

\textbf{Downstream utility.}
$k$NN classification independently confirms that the learned representations are discriminatively useful: Topic-FM-Transformer's accuracy (0.733) and macro-F1 (0.517) exceed every Pure-VAE variant by 13--22\%.

\textbf{Built-in interpretability.}
Unlike prior-free VAEs, the Dirichlet decoder contains an explicit topic--gene matrix $\beta$ whose rows map topics to gene-expression probabilities. This supports dual-pathway biological validation---perturbation importance and direct $\beta$-weight readout---both yielding significant GO enrichment (Bonferroni $p < 0.05$) on endoderm and dentate datasets. The flow matching module preserves this property because it refines the pre-softmax representation without modifying $\beta$.

\textbf{External positioning.}
Among 23 external baselines, scVI~\cite{lopez2018deep} is the only model with higher NMI/ARI than Topic-FM-Transformer on individual datasets, and scDAC~\cite{li2023scdac} is the only model with higher ASW. Neither matches the combined concordance--geometry--interpretability--downstream profile of Topic-FM. Topic-FM-Contrastive's 86.4\% core win rate positions it as the strongest overall model in the comparison.

\textbf{Limitations.}
The simplex constraint makes DRE/LSE distance-preservation metrics non-comparable across prior families and limits the latent space to $K{-}1$ effective dimensions. The number of topics ($K{=}10$) is fixed and may not suit all biological systems; automatic selection of $K$ via held-out likelihood or stability analysis remains future work. The flow matching warmup ($T_\text{warm}{=}50$) introduces an additional hyperparameter, though sensitivity analysis shows broad robustness. Six of the 23 external baselines produced zero performance on multiple datasets and were excluded from win-rate calculations.
