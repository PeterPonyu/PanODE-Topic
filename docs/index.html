Abstract

Simplex topics, then a locked split

From the MDPI manuscript. This page does not headline a Topic-FM NMI/ARI win. Wilcoxon scope stays 11 externals × 37 metrics.

Variational autoencoders for single-cell transcriptomics typically learn Gaussian latent spaces that lack part-based interpretability: individual latent dimensions carry no inherent biological meaning and the decoder provides no explicit gene-program readout. We introduce Topic-FM, a family of neural topic VAEs in which a logistic-normal Dirichlet prior constrains the latent vector to the probability simplex, turning each coordinate into a topic proportion and the decoder weight matrix into a directly readable topic–gene signature. A conditional optimal-transport flow field, trained entirely in pre-softmax ℝK, sharpens posterior geometry without modifying the decoder or breaking simplex validity.

16-core ablation lock. On the verified 16-core ablation export, Pure-VAE leads mean NMI (0.564) and ARI (0.363), whereas Topic-FM-Transformer leads ASW (0.501) and DAV (0.763) — a concordance–geometry split, not uniform dominance. No in-repo 56-dataset mean file supports a claim that Topic-FM wins all metrics versus Pure-VAE.

12-dataset Wilcoxon, 11 named externals. A separate 12-dataset Wilcoxon export tests matched encoder pairs (Topic-FM vs. Pure-*-VAE) and does not override those 16-core means. Against 11 named externals (N_pairs = 12; 407 = 11 × 37 metric rows) the external table is a scoped signed-rank screen, not a 56 × 18 bake-off.

Downstream kNN and programs. Downstream kNN classification on 12 datasets is higher for Topic-FM-Transformer than Pure-VAE (accuracy 0.733 vs. 0.646; macro-F1 0.517 vs. 0.405). Dual-pathway biological validation — perturbation importance and direct decoder-β readout — yields convergent GO enrichment, demonstrating that the learned topics correspond to coherent, annotatable gene programs rather than opaque embedding dimensions. Topic-GAT has no restored biology panel.
